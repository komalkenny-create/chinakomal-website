Deepwater and Gas-Condensate Drilling Move Solids Control and High-Pressure Pumps Up the Specification List

November 4, 2026

BEIJING, China – CHINA KOMAL INTERNATIONAL CO., LTD reports a shift in the way international drilling contractors are specifying mud circulation and solids control equipment, as deepwater exploration and high-rate gas-condensate developments expand the operating envelope beyond what many onshore-derived specifications were written for. According to industry analysis, a growing share of new rig programmes now originates from offshore and near-offshore acreage where narrow pressure windows, high mud weights, and strict environmental discharge rules all meet at the same time.

The practical consequence for equipment buyers is straightforward: circulation hardware that was adequate for vertical development wells on land is frequently under-specified for a modern offshore or subsea-tieback programme. CHINA KOMAL's engineering team notes that this is less about horsepower than about margin — margin on pressure rating, margin on wear life, and margin on the drilling fluid properties the surface system must handle without degradation.

"When an operator moves from land development drilling to a high-pressure gas well, the surface circulation system becomes part of the risk profile, not just a utility," said CHINA KOMAL's technical sales manager for international drilling projects. "Narrow pressure windows leave very little tolerance for fluctuation in mud weight, and that places direct demands on the pumps, the shakers, and the degassing equipment that sit between the well and the pit."

High-Pressure Mud Pumps for Narrow Pressure Windows

CHINA KOMAL has recorded rising specification of its KM series high-pressure mud pumps and the 3NB-1600 class of triplex pumps for deep and high-pressure wells, where operators are running heavier mud systems and require pressure stability across long circulating intervals rather than peak output on a single pump stroke. The company's high-pressure pump line extends from compact 75 kW units used on workover and well-servicing rigs through to large frames suited to deep drilling programmes, allowing contractors to standardise on one supplier family across a mixed fleet.

The company also reports that fluid end specification is being reviewed at the same time as the pump itself. Buyers are increasingly requesting matched sets — cylinder liners, piston assemblies, valve bodies and seats, and intermediate rods — for Gardner Denver PZ and TGE series, OILWELL A1700PT, EMSCO FB1600 and IDECO T1600 platforms, rather than procuring individual components as they fail. According to CHINA KOMAL, the logic is economic: a planned rebuild during a scheduled maintenance window costs a fraction of the same work done under rig-time pressure after an unplanned failure.

"Heavy mud is unforgiving on the fluid end," the technical sales manager explained. "Higher solids loading and higher density accelerate liner and valve wear, and the failure mode is rarely gradual. Customers moving into these conditions are asking us for documented material and heat-treatment standards, and for the wear parts to be supplied as a matched set from the same production batch."

Solids Control Under Higher Mud Weights

Higher mud weight places a corresponding load on the solids control train. CHINA KOMAL reports increased interest in its shale shaker and mud cleaner lines, and in composite-frame shaker screens configured for Brandt B40, D380, King Cobra and LCM-2D shakers, Derrick DP-600 and FLC-2000 series, and Kemtron and FSI configurations. Contractors running heavy mud systems are also asking for screen selection support at the start of a campaign, since screen type, mesh range, and shaker motion all affect how much solids reach the downstream desander and centrifuge stages.

Alongside screening, the company points to gas-condensate operations as a specific driver of demand for its mud gas separator and centrifugal degasser range. In gas-bearing intervals, even modest amounts of entrained gas in the returns can destabilise hydrostatic pressure if it is not removed before the mud returns to the active system. CHINA KOMAL's degasser and separator packages are being specified together with the shaker and mud cleaner stages so that the entire train is sized as one system rather than as separate items on a purchase order.

"Solids control is where a heavy mud programme either holds together or falls apart," said CHINA KOMAL's solids control engineering lead. "If the shakers are under-screened, the desilter and centrifuge carry a load they were never sized for, and the whole train loses efficiency at exactly the moment a narrow pressure window gives you no room to lose it. That is why we now push for a full solids control review before a campaign, not just a screen quotation."

Certification, Documentation, and Inventory

Offshore-led procurement brings documentation requirements that onshore buyers have historically treated as secondary. CHINA KOMAL reports that international contractors are requesting material traceability, pressure-test records, and dimensional inspection reports as standard deliverables with high-pressure pump and fluid end orders, and are increasingly consolidating these requirements into supplier qualification rather than reviewing them case by case.

To support the installed base, the company maintains buffer inventory of high-turnover items — fluid end parts, shaker screens, and common pump spares — at stocking points linked to the major operating regions it serves across the Middle East, Central Asia, North America, South America and Southeast Asia. Standard replacement items can typically be dispatched from regional stock within days of order confirmation, with application support available remotely and on-site for commissioning and solids control optimisation.

The company also works with contractors on crew-level training covering pump inspection, fluid end rebuild procedure, and screen selection, with the objective of making each rig team self-sufficient between major overhauls and reducing the dependency on supplier visits that remote or offshore locations make expensive.

"The contractors winning these programmes are the ones who treat the mud system as engineering rather than procurement," the technical sales manager concluded. "They specify for the conditions they expect to meet on the hardest well in the campaign, not the average well, and they plan their parts inventory around the maintenance schedule rather than the failure schedule. That is the discipline the market is rewarding, and it is where we are putting our engineering and our stock."
