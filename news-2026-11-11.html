Mixed Rig Fleets Put Interchangeability at the Centre of Solids Control and Pump Spares Planning

November 11, 2026

BEIJING, China – CHINA KOMAL INTERNATIONAL CO., LTD reports that the hardest procurement problem facing many international drilling contractors in 2027 is not selecting equipment for a new rig, but keeping a fleet of rigs built in different decades running on one spares budget. As rig contractors consolidate after a period of acquisition, bought-in rigs arrive with whatever surface package they were delivered with — Brandt shakers on one location, Derrick on another, SWACO, Kemtron and FSI units elsewhere, and mud pumps from Gardner Denver, OILWELL, EMSCO and IDECO mixed across the same fleet.

The operational effect is well understood on the rig but rarely priced properly at head office. Every additional make of screen, liner, valve and seat is another part number to stock, another minimum order quantity to carry, another item that can be out of stock on the location where it is actually needed. CHINA KOMAL's engineered products team argues that interchangeability — the ability to cover several makes from a single, well-documented part range — has become as important a purchasing criterion as unit price.

"Contractors do not come to us saying they want the cheapest screen on the market," said CHINA KOMAL's export manager for solids control products. "They say they have eleven rigs, five screen makes and no idea which of them will be down next week. What they are buying is the ability to plan. Interchangeability is what turns a long parts list into a manageable one."

Shaker Screens: The Widest Part Range in the Surface System

Shaker screens are the most frequently replaced consumable in the solids control train and, consequently, the item where a scattered installed base hurts most. A contractor running Brandt B40, D380 and King Cobra shakers, Derrick DP-600 and FLC-2000 series, SWACO Mongoose and Meerkat units, plus Kemtron and FSI configurations, is nominally stocking five or six distinct screen families before mesh selection is even considered.

CHINA KOMAL supplies composite-frame screen ranges configured to those shaker platforms, including Brandt LCM-2D, VSM-300 primary and secondary, and Hyperpool wave and PWP patterns, Derrick FLC-500 and FLC-2000 wave and PWP patterns, SWACO MD2 and MD3, Derrick DP-600 and Vortex Orbital 3000 formats. The company's approach is to hold the dimensional envelope and API RP 13C performance documentation for each pattern, so that a contractor can standardise the mesh ladder used across several makes of shaker rather than maintaining a different mesh map per shaker brand.

That standardisation is not only a stock-keeping convenience. Where crews move between rigs and shaker makes rotate between locations, a consistent mesh convention reduces the chance of running a screen finer or coarser than the drilling programme intended — a failure mode that quietly shows up later as excessive dilution, higher mud cost or a desilter that fouls unexpectedly.

"The screen is where a solids control decision is actually made, every few hours, by whoever is on the shaker," said CHINA KOMAL's solids control engineering lead. "If that person has to translate between four different shaker makes on three different rigs, mistakes happen. If the mesh convention is the same across the fleet, the decision is the same. That is worth more than the price difference between two screen suppliers."

Fluid End Parts: Matched Sets Across Pump Families

The same problem appears more expensively on the mud pump side. Contractors operating Gardner Denver PZ and TGE series pumps alongside OILWELL A1700PT, EMSCO FB1600 and IDECO T1600 units maintain separate inventory for cylinder liners, piston assemblies, valve bodies and seats, intermediate rods and piston rods for each family. CHINA KOMAL manufactures fluid end parts for those platforms and, increasingly, supplies them as matched sets drawn from a single production batch so that wear rates across the components of a rebuild are consistent.

According to the company, mixing parts from different batches or of uncertain provenance is one of the more common causes of premature fluid end failure — a liner that wears unevenly against a piston assembly of slightly different hardness, or a valve and seat that do not bed correctly and wash cut in a fraction of their expected life. Supplying a rebuild as a documented set, with material and heat-treatment records attached, removes that variable.

For workover and well-servicing contractors the calculation is tighter still. The company's smaller frame pumps — the KM series from 75 kW to 450 kW — are widely used on workover rigs where a pump failure during a round trip carries a direct rig-time cost, and where the local supply of parts may be days away. CHINA KOMAL reports that buyers ordering workover rig packages are increasingly requesting a spares kit scoped to the maintenance schedule rather than a single spare of each component.

"A fluid end rebuild is predictable maintenance," the export manager said. "You know roughly how many hours a set of liners will run in your conditions. The question is whether the parts are on the location when those hours are up. Contractors who plan a rebuild get a scheduled cost. Contractors who wait for a failure get a rig-time cost, and those are not the same number."

Sizing the Train as One System, Not Five Parts

Alongside parts interchangeability, CHINA KOMAL points to package-level sizing as the second lever contractors are pulling. Where the desander, desilter, mud cleaner, gas separator and centrifuge stages were bought separately over the life of a rig, the train frequently ends up with stages whose capacities no longer match the mud programme the rig is now running — a mud cleaner sized for an older, lighter programme feeding a centrifuge selected for a different purpose entirely.

The company's practice is to review the entire train when a contractor is refurbishing a surface package or adding a rig to a fleet, sizing each stage against the actual flow rate, mud weight and the solids control objective for the programme. That review typically covers shale shakers, mud cleaners, desanders and desilters, vacuum and centrifugal degassers, mud gas separators, decanter and vertical cuttings dryers, and the transfer and charge pumps that link them.

"Solids control is a chain, and a chain is sized by its weakest link, not by its best component," the solids control engineering lead said. "When we are asked to quote a mud cleaner for a rig, we usually end up asking about the shaker upstream and the centrifuge downstream. Half the time the right answer is not the item that was on the purchase requisition."

Documentation as a Procurement Requirement

CHINA KOMAL reports that the documentation around spares has moved from a formality to a supplier qualification criterion. Contractors auditing a mixed fleet need to know, for each part, which platform it fits, what material and heat treatment it was made to, and what inspection records exist. For screens, that includes the dimensional envelope and performance data under API RP 13C; for fluid end parts, pressure-test and material traceability records.

The company maintains buffer inventory of high-turnover items — shaker screens, fluid end parts and common pump spares — at stocking points linked to the operating regions it serves across the Middle East, Central Asia, North America, South America and Southeast Asia, with dispatch from regional stock typically within days of order confirmation. Application support for screen selection, fluid end rebuild procedure and full-train solids control review is available remotely, and on-site for commissioning and crew training.

"The fleet audit is where this starts," the export manager concluded. "Sit down with the list of every rig, every shaker and every pump, and ask how many part numbers you are actually carrying to keep them all running. Most contractors are surprised by the number, and relieved by how far it can come down when the supply side is organised around interchangeability instead of around individual brands."
