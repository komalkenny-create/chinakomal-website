Iraq's Southern Field Development and Gas Expansion Drive Demand for Workover Rigs, High-Pressure Mud Pumps, Fluid End Parts and Solids Control

October 21, 2026

BEIJING, China – CHINA KOMAL INTERNATIONAL CO., LTD reports rising engagement with contractors, EPC firms and equipment rental companies serving Iraq's upstream oil and gas sector, as the country's southern fields, associated gas programme and brownfield redevelopment activity continue to absorb oilfield equipment at scale. According to industry analysis, Iraqi procurement activity is being shaped by three converging forces: sustained production support from the mature fields around Basrah, continued development drilling and workover activity required to offset natural decline, and a growing focus on reducing routine gas flaring through associated gas gathering and processing projects.

Service companies working in and around the Basrah, Maysan, Dhi Qar and Wasit hubs are managing high well counts, rising water cuts and long, hot operating seasons. That combination places steady demand on workover rigs, matched fluid end consumables and dependable high-pressure pumping capacity—the fast-moving items that decide whether a rig keeps turning during a short maintenance window.

"Iraq is a market where the rig schedule is set months ahead and the storage yard is the real bottleneck," said CHINA KOMAL's regional sales director for the Middle East. "There is no waiting for a part to clear customs twice. Contractors here want a documented specification, a firm lead time and a spare set on the shelf before the crew leaves the yard. Our job is to make procurement boring."

Mature Southern Fields Keep Workover and Pump Demand Firm

A large share of Iraq's production comes from fields that have been on stream for decades, many of them under extended development contracts with international partners. Natural decline, water breakthrough and the need to restore shut-in wells keep workover rigs and well-servicing units busy across the south. That operating rhythm drives predictable consumption of matched fluid end kits—pistons, cylinder liners, valve bodies and seats, and packings for scheduled pump rebuilds—as well as shaker screens for the shale shakers, mud cleaners and decanter centrifuges that anchor solids control on land rigs and workover units.

CHINA KOMAL reports growing orders for its composite-frame shaker screen line, covering Brandt (B40, D380, King Cobra, LCM-2D) and Derrick (DP-600, FLC-2000, Hyperpool) configurations, as well as Swaco and Kemtron patterns used extensively across regional rig fleets. Because contractors working in Iraq typically standardise on a narrow set of screen types to simplify logistics and crew training, screen supply has become a recurring theme in inquiries from the region. The company also reports steady demand for workover rigs and related lifting equipment, including crown blocks, traveling blocks, hooks and swivels, which are consumed heavily across active well-service fleets.

Associated Gas and Brownfield Projects Lift Higher-Specification Pumping

Alongside production support, Iraq's associated gas gathering and processing programme is broadening the equipment picture. Well-intervention, cementing, workover and completion activity tied to gas capture and processing projects all depend on high-pressure pumping capacity that performs predictably across long, cost-sensitive campaigns where a single equipment failure can mean costly rig standby and missed milestones.

CHINA KOMAL has recorded increasing interest in its KM series high-pressure mud pumps for drilling, workover and intervention applications, available across a range of power ratings to match different rig and unit classes. The pumps are being specified alongside the company's fluid end parts programme covering widely used triplex and quintuplex configurations, including parts matching Gardner Denver PZ and TGE series pumps as well as IDECO, OILWELL and EMSCO platforms common across the region's service fleet. A common request from Iraqi buyers is a complete spare parts package shipped as a single line item, so that a rig crew has a full rebuild set on hand rather than ordering component by component.

"In a brownfield well, the specification is fixed before spud and a failure at depth costs days rather than hours," the regional sales director explained. "Contractors in Iraq treat part quality as a risk-management decision rather than a line-item cost. That is driving a clear shift toward certified components with material traceability and repeatable performance—and toward suppliers who can deliver the entire rebuild kit rather than ninety percent of it."

Solids Control Economics in a Hot, Abrasive and Water-Intensive Environment

Iraq's geology—thick evaporite sections, reactive shales, abrasive sands and pressure transitions in the same hole—places particular demands on solids control. Effective screen selection, together with mud cleaner and decanter centrifuge performance, is central to drilling efficiency, mud costs and waste volumes, especially where water for mud make-up is expensive to transport and difficult to dispose of.

Heat is an additional engineering factor in the south of the country. Surface temperatures through the summer months shorten elastomer life, raise lubricant consumption and put thermal stress on fluid end components and screen frames. CHINA KOMAL's engineering team reports that selection guidance for the region leans toward higher-temperature elastomers where service conditions warrant, composite-frame screens with documented mesh counts and consistent conductance, and fluid end components that hold their tolerances through abrasive service. The regional team pairs screen recommendations with cuttings analysis at the start of each campaign so that contractors can match mesh and screen type to the formation rather than defaulting to a single specification.

Regional Stocking, Support and Lifecycle Partnerships

To serve a market that stretches from the Basrah hub through the southern oilfields to Baghdad and the northern fields, CHINA KOMAL is strengthening its regional logistics arrangements, maintaining buffer inventory of high-turnover items including fluid end parts, shaker screens and common pump spares at stocking points with reliable air, road and sea access to major operating centres. The company reports that standard replacement items can be dispatched from regional stock within days of order confirmation, with application support available remotely and on-site for rig-up, commissioning and solids control optimisation.

The company is also working with regional service companies to build local maintenance capability, providing training on pump inspection, fluid end rebuild procedures and screen selection practice. CHINA KOMAL says the objective is to help operators and contractors stay self-sufficient between major overhauls, with the regional team serving as a technical backstop rather than a dependency—an approach proven across its expanding international operations.

"The companies winning in Iraq are those that plan the spare parts yard as carefully as the drilling programme," the regional sales director concluded. "From mature southern fields to associated gas and brownfield development, our job is to put world-class pumps, parts and screens in our customers' hands and back them with inventory and expertise that match the scale of their operations. In a market this demanding, uptime is the product—and that is what we deliver."
